## What to build

1. **Heights, optional fields, in metres in the file** (added to the 6.5 schema version's list in
   docs/SBD_FORMAT.md under "Added to this version by later 6.5 milestones"; no new version, no migration: a
   missing prop means "not set"):
   - the room (`plan` of kind `room`): `ceiling_height_m`;
   - a window: `sill_height_m` (floor to the bottom of the glass) and `top_height_m` (floor to its top);
   - a door: `height_m` (if doors or other items already carry a height prop for the label "a height that's set
     shows as a small label", use that same prop and name and say so; don't make a second one).
   Edited in the Selection tile as lengths, shown in metres or feet as the plan's units say, typed in either
   (length.ts). Checked: positive; a window's top above its sill; neither above the ceiling when one is set (a
   plain inline message, the value still refused); cleared by emptying the field. Each change an op. They draw
   nothing on the plan and print nothing: they are data for 3D. Exported from src/core/model through one reader
   (`roomHeights`/`openingHeights` or the like), documented as what `get_plan` and the 3D set will read.
2. **Which way a window faces, and whether its wall is an outside wall.** One pure function in src/core/model
   (e.g. `windowSun.ts`), from the plan's walls and the room's north:
   - A window has two sides. From its middle, a ray along each of the wall's two normals: a side whose ray meets
     no other wall of the room (walls as planSightlines.ts sees them, the window's own wall left out) is open to
     the outside. Exactly one open side: an outside wall, facing that way. Both sides meet walls: an inside wall,
     no sun (says "Inside wall"). Both open (a free-standing wall, a room not closed yet): it can't tell which
     side is outside: says so ("Close the room to tell which side is outside") and gives no hours.
   - The facing as a compass bearing through the room's north, and as a word (N, NE, E…).
   - No north: says north is missing, no hours.
3. **The hours.** For an outside window, with the sun's place, date and zone from 6.5.5's reading rule: the
   intervals between sunrise and sunset when the sun is above the horizon and within 90° of the window's outward
   direction (in front of the glass), worked out from the sun's azimuth and altitude minute by minute (SunCalc,
   as sunPath.ts uses it), given as local times in the location's zone, rounded to 5 minutes: "Direct sun: 07:40
   to 11:15". None that day: "No direct sun on [date]". At high latitudes two intervals can occur: list both.
   Sill and top heights, when set, add the sun's altitude range to the reader's result for 3D later but do not
   change the hours (with no overhang, any sun in front of the glass comes through).
   - Always with the line: "Buildings and trees outside aren't known." (en; es through t()).
   - Shown: in the Selection tile for a selected window; and as a short list for every outside window of the room
     in the place the compass opens (6.5.5), each named by its facing ("Window, facing SE: 07:40 to 11:15").
   - Computed, never stored (a rollup); cached per records object if it is read on every edit (recordIndex.ts's
     pattern), and it must not make a plan edit scan every record.
   - The reader's result type is exported and documented for the MCP (`get_plan`) and the 3D set.
4. Nothing is drawn on the plan in this brief (no light patches on the floor): say in the report whether that
   would be a small addition, for Kevin to decide.

## Round 2 (from the review; the four rules at the top still come first; the Edit tool only)

1. MEDIUM. A window facing another wing of the same room (a U-shaped or courtyard room, a recessed entrance) must
   not read "Inside wall". Rule, to state in the comment: along each normal count the walls the ray CROSSES (all
   of them, not the first hit; the window's own wall left out; two walls sharing a corner the ray passes exactly
   through count once). A side with no crossing is outside (as now). If both sides cross walls: exactly one side
   with an EVEN count is the outside (a ray leaving a closed room crosses an odd number of its walls; from just
   outside, an even number); both odd is an inside wall; anything else (both even) can't be told ("Close the
   room…" wording, or a new line "Can't tell which side of this window is outside": choose and say). Tests: the
   reviewer's U shape (walls round (0,0)→(2,0)→(2,2)→(4,2)→(4,0)→(6,0)→(6,4)→(0,4)→(0,0), a window in
