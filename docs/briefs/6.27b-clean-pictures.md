## Why (checkpoint nov17-627, Mac WebKit)

scoutPhotos.spec "…what is stored has no EXIF" failed on the Mac: WebKit's JPEG encoder writes its own blocks into
every JPEG the page encodes. Decoded from the run's trace, the stored picture carried:
- APP1 "Exif": an Exif IFD with only PixelXDimension 640 and PixelYDimension 345 (346 for the second photo);
- APP13 "Photoshop 3.0": an empty IPTC record (8BIM 0x0404) and its digest (8BIM 0x0425, MD5 of nothing);
- APP2 ICC_PROFILE: Display P3, © Apple 2022 (the colour; it must stay).
No date or place, but "stored with no EXIF" was false on WebKit, and a later webview could add more.

## Kevin's decisions (Nov 18, night)

- **One clean, in TypeScript, in the page's one encoder**: `canvasBlob` in src/core/media/canvas.ts. Every picture
  the page encodes passes through it: what is stored (picture and thumbnail), and what leaves in a deck PDF, a PNG,
  a plan export, a swatch strip, a call sheet's map or logo. Not a Rust clean plus a TypeScript one: with one clean,
  WebKit's tests run the code that ships.
- **Every encode goes through canvasBlob.** Known ones that don't today: src/app/video.ts (`canvas.toBlob(…,
  'image/png')` in `still`) and src/app/company/companies.ts (`canvas.toDataURL('image/png')`). Route them through
  canvasBlob (a data URL can be made from the cleaned blob). Grep for any other `toBlob`, `convertToBlob`,
  `toDataURL` or other encode outside canvas.ts, route each, and list every one in your report.
- **Keep by a list, never remove by a list.** Keep only what a reader needs to show the picture, plus the colour
  profile; every other block goes, so whatever a later webview adds goes too.
  - JPEG keeps: SOI; APP0 JFIF; APP2 ICC_PROFILE (all its chunks, in order); APP14 Adobe (it changes how colour
    decodes); DQT, DHT, DRI, every SOFn, SOS and its entropy-coded data (with RSTn markers inside it), EOI. Every
    other marker segment goes (APP1 Exif and XMP, APP13, other APPn, COM).
  - PNG keeps: IHDR, PLTE, tRNS, IDAT, IEND, and the colour chunks iCCP, sRGB, cICP, gAMA, cHRM. Every other chunk
    goes (tEXt, zTXt, iTXt, eXIf, tIME, pHYs and any unknown one). CRCs are kept as they were (each kept chunk is
    copied whole).
  - Write the keep-list in docs/SBD_FORMAT.md, and make its EXIF sentence (around line 332, "its bytes carry no EXIF
    at all") say exactly what the stored bytes carry: the picture's own data and its colour profile, nothing else.
    Also line 140's `bytes` row if it needs it.
- **Bytes the clean doesn't expect** (not a JPEG or PNG, a segment running past the end, a missing SOS or IDAT, any
  parse surprise): nothing is stored or exported, and the person is told the picture couldn't be saved (a new
  translation key in en.ts and es.ts). They are never stored as they came. The clean never throws past its caller
  unhandled; the parse is bounded by the bytes' length.
- **The hash is taken after the clean** (src/core/media/store.ts, image.ts), and `isTaggedP3` (image.ts) must still
  find the profile in the cleaned bytes.
- **Rust refuses at the door**, written separately (its own Rust code, the same keep-list): in src-tauri/src/sbd.rs,
  a transaction (`sbd_tx`, `sbd_tx_raw`) whose statement writes `media_files` or `thumbnails` with a blob that is a
  JPEG or PNG (by its first bytes) carrying any block outside the keep-list is refused whole, nothing written, with
  an error the page shows as "couldn't be saved". Not on files kept as they are: a PDF attachment, an issued call
  sheet's PDF (not JPEG or PNG, so the check doesn't apply). If you find an image the app stores as it came on
  purpose, stop and report it.
- **The test stand-in refuses the same way**: src/core/storage/memoryDriver.ts (sql.js, dev only) gets the same door
  check, in TypeScript, so a test of an unclean write fails as the app would.
- **Pictures an older build stored stay as they are**: their hash is their name. They must still open, and still
  come back through Import project data (JSON, src/app/projectJsonFile.ts and src/app/projects/actions.ts). How the
  refusal lets them through: the JSON import writes through its own Rust command (for one: `sbd_import_tx_raw`, the
  same packing as `sbd_tx_raw`), which writes pictures as they came; the ordinary writes keep the door. The memory
  driver mirrors the split. The new command goes into lib.rs, src/app/tauriContract.test.ts and a Rust command test
  (tauri's `test` feature, as the others in src-tauri/src/command_tests.rs). If the import's current path makes this
  wrong or much bigger than it sounds, stop and report what you found before building it.

## Additions (Kevin, Nov 18, late), sent to the builder mid-run

1. The keep-list holds for the whole file: between a JPEG's scans too (only DHT, DQT, DRI, SOFn, SOS, RSTn and the
   keep-list's APPn between scans), and nothing after the end marker: the clean cuts every byte after JPEG EOI or PNG
   IEND, and the door (Rust and the memory driver) refuses a JPEG or PNG with bytes after its end.
2. The door decides by what the bytes are, never by the type the row claims: any blob that starts as a JPEG or a PNG
   is checked, whatever its mime column says.
3. The colour profile is the one block kept without being written by us: at most 1 MiB in total (all APP2 chunks
   together; a PNG iCCP's compressed and inflated sizes both at most 1 MiB). The clean and the door refuse above it;
   SBD_FORMAT.md states the limit beside the keep-list; tests just under and just over.
4. The import's own door checks each picture's sha256 against SHA-256 of its bytes and refuses the import whole on a
   mismatch, with words. SBD_FORMAT.md says in one sentence that pictures brought in by Import project data are
   stored as they came, checked against their hash.
