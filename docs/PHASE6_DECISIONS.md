# Phase 6 decisions (asked Nov 13, 2026; answered by Kevin Nov 13: go)

One page. Each decision: the question, what was decided and how hard it is to change later. "As recommended" means
the recommendation Kevin was shown is the decision, written here in full. The plan is in `docs/PLAN.md`, "Phase 6
milestones"; the scope is "Phase 6 notes" there (Kevin, Nov 3) and CLAUDE.md's bird's-eye rules.

| # | Question | Decided (Kevin, Nov 13) | Change later |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How is a plan stored? | As recommended. **A record per item**, `plan_item` under its `plan`, as boards are (Phase 5, decision 1): every move an op, so undo, History, the Trash, suggestions and the MCP work unchanged. Positions and sizes in **metres**, always, whatever the screen shows. | Hard: it's the file format. |
| 2 | Does every setup redraw the room? | **Kevin's: three levels, each drawn once and shown live, never copied.** (a) **The room** (walls, doors, windows, set pieces, the underlay and its scale, north) is its own record that scenes point to. A scene starts from a new room or one already drawn (rooms at its location first), so a set in three scenes is one room and a moved wall moves in all of them. "Make a separate copy" is for a scene that needs its own version. (b) **Blocking** (cast marks and paths) belongs to the scene and shows in each of its setups. (c) **Cameras, lights, grip, sound and production items** belong to the setup. A new setup starts empty or from another setup's state, copied, since most of a scene's lighting carries over. (d) Anything can also be placed **in one setup only** (a cheated mark, an extra set piece). Stored without a new record type (decision 16): each level is a `plan` record whose `kind` is `room` (under the project, pointed to by scenes' `room_id`), `blocking` (under its scene) or `setup` (under its setup); an item's level is the plan it sits in. | Hard once plans exist. |
| 3 | What is a setup, and how do frames join one? | As recommended, with Kevin's numbering. **A `setup` record under its scene**, **numbered within the scene (Setup 1, 2, 3), never lettered** (shot numbers already use letters: 12A), named by hand if wanted. The number comes from its order, never stored. A frame joins at most one (`setup_id`, which Shoot days' totals already read); frames with none stay setups of their own. New setup from selected frames on the board, or in the Setup workspace. | Medium. |
| 4 | Where do camera bodies live? | As recommended. **A camera body is a `gear_item`** with the sensor width and height in mm of the recording mode used (full frame, Super 35 crop, open gate) and a letter (A cam, B cam). A table in the code offers common bodies and modes, always editable by hand. Kevin's: **keep the table short, each entry from the maker's own spec page**, plus one entry for phones and anything else known by equivalent focal lengths: **36 × 24 mm, labelled "Focal lengths as full-frame equivalents"**. The project's first body is the default for every camera on a plan. | Medium. |
| 5 | Zooms and anamorphic lenses | As recommended, with the correction under 6. **A lens is a `gear_item`** with focal range (prime: one number) and squeeze (1 by default; 1.33, 1.5, 1.8, 2). A frame may pick its lens (`lens_id`); `lens_mm` stays the focal length used and must sit in the range. A frame with no lens picked works as today, squeeze 1. | Medium. |
| 6 | Which lens does a camera on the plan draw? | As recommended: **live from the frame** (views never copy data). A camera is linked to the frames shot from it and draws the picked frame's field of view; a camera with no frame yet has a lens typed on it, and the check "storyboard lens differs from the plan's camera" fires only when a frame linked later disagrees. **Kevin's correction: the cone shows what the project's frame sees, not the whole sensor.** Take the recording mode's area after de-squeeze (width × squeeze, by height), fit the project's frame shape (`aspect_ratio`) inside it, and use that width: field of view = 2 × atan(width / (2 × focal length)). A camera has an **"On its side"** switch for vertical shooting, which swaps the mode's width and height (built in 6.1 as: de-squeeze first, then turn, since the squeeze turns with the camera; 35.6 × 20.0, 2×, 2.39:1, on its side, 50 mm reads 20.0 mm, 22.6°; Kevin to confirm). Tests, on a 35.6 × 20.0 mm mode: 50 mm, 2× squeeze, 2.39:1 frame: 47.8 mm, 51.1° (not 70.9°). 24 mm, no squeeze, 9:16 frame: 11.25 mm, 26.4°; on its side, 20.0 mm, 45.2°. | Hard once plans rely on copies. |
| 7 | Metres or feet | As recommended. **This computer's, by region** (feet and inches in the US, metres elsewhere), with a Settings override, as weather units are. The file is always metres. Lengths can be typed either way ("12ft", "3.5m") and, Kevin's, **as feet and inches (12' 6")**. Grid 0.5 m or 1 ft; a scale bar always on the plan. | Easy. |
| 8 | Lights, flags and diffusion | As recommended. **A library from the 68 plan icons in the kit**, by department, each with real default sizes. A light has a beam angle drawn as a cone, a name and, if wanted, the `gear_item` it is, so a light the day's gear list lacks is found by the existing gear check. Flags and diffusion have real widths (4×4, 8×8, 12×12…). Kevin's: **a light also carries its watts (from its gear item, or typed), and the legend totals the setup's watts.** | Easy. |
| 9 | Cast marks and movement | As recommended, **at the scene's level** (see 2). **A mark per character** (the scene's `character` records), numbered positions 1, 2, 3 with a path between them, built on Phase 5's connectors. A camera move is the same: positions with a path (a dolly track has its real length), at the setup's level with its camera. | Medium. |
| 10 | Department layers | As recommended. **A focus view, never access control** (as 3.6c). An item's layer comes from what it is: camera, lighting, grip, art, cast blocking. Each layer shows, hides, dims and locks; a person starts on their own department's layer with the others dimmed. | Easy. |
| 11 | Heights | As recommended. **Keep them now, draw them later:** wall height, a camera's height (a frame's `height` from 3.18 when linked), a light's height, as plain fields in metres; nothing drawn in 3D. Kevin's: **a height that's set shows as a small label by its item and in the legend.** | Easy now, a migration later. |
| 12 | A floor plan or location photo underneath | As recommended. **A picture underneath, set to scale by two points and a typed distance**, locked and dimmed; stored as board pictures are (2560 px with a pointer to the original). A measure tool reads any distance. Kevin's: **the underlay belongs to the room.** | Easy. |
| 13 | Is a plan themed? | As recommended. **Never themed, as boards (Kevin, Nov 11):** the plan is paper with ink in light and dark, department colours fixed, so it prints and reads the same everywhere. Only the app around it follows the theme. | Easy. |
| 14 | Getting a plan out | As recommended. **PDF and PNG per setup** through the export registry: the plan to scale with a scale bar, a legend by department, the setup's frames and lenses, the scene heading. Kevin's: **two packets, each one PDF: a scene (a page per setup) and a shoot day (every setup with a frame that day, in the day's order). Paper by region, as call sheets.** On the call sheet and the web: later. | Easy. |
| 15 | How fast | As recommended. **300 items, pan, zoom and drag at 60 fps** on Kevin's Mac and a mid Windows laptop, measured as 5.7's board was. | Easy. |
| 16 | The file format | **Approved: props only, one schema version for everything these answers need.** No new table and no new record type: `setup`, `plan` and `plan_item` exist. Every new prop is listed in SBD_FORMAT.md and in the report to Kevin; a backup before the upgrade; older files open unchanged. **A new table or record type still waits for Kevin.** | Hard. |

Added by Kevin, Nov 13: **north and the sun**, a milestone after 6.5, making two kit icons live.
- **North:** one per room; turning it sets the room's north.
- **Sun path:** when the setup's frames have a location and a shoot day, it draws the real sun for that place and
  date (SunCalc, in the location's time zone): the arc from sunrise to sunset with the hours marked, and the sun's
  direction and height at a time set on the item. With no location, day or north, it says what's missing and draws
  nothing. Tested against published values (NOAA's solar calculator, say) for a known place, date and time; tests
  never call a live service.
